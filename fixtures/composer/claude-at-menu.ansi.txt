  executable invoked under a second name. The relationship survives intact.   [0m[38;5;153mvi[0m shipped with 1BSD in 1978 and became the Unix default; Mark Horton later maintained it and broadened terminal support. Its modal design came from scarcity — over a 300-baud line, every
  redrawn character cost real time, so normal mode turned the whole keyboard into commands.

  Emacs took the opposite path. Richard Stallman and Guy Steele collected a set of Editor MACroS for TECO in 1976 into something modeless and extensible all the way down. GNU Emacs, released in
  1985 with a C core and an Emacs Lisp interpreter, let users redefine any part of the editor while it ran.

  [0m[1mGraphical editors[0m

  Xerox PARC went a third way. Bravo, from Charles Simonyi and Butler Lampson in 1974, showed formatted text as it would print; Gypsy added modeless selection and cut, copy, and paste. That line
  runs to WordStar and Word, and it split the field: word processors for documents, text editors for code. BBEdit followed on the Mac in 1992, TextMate in 2004 with language bundles, Sublime Text
  in 2008 with a fast core and multiple cursors. Vim, Bram Moolenaar's 1991 reimplementation of [0m[38;5;153mvi[0m, became the version almost everyone actually ran.
